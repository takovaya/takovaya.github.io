Research

Periplus literature, ancient geography, and the cognitive shape of spatial knowledge

Ancient geography has been studied extensively as a body of geographical knowledge. Much less attention has been paid to the cognitive processes that made such knowledge possible: how sailors perceived space, organised routes in memory and transformed movement into written description. My research focuses on that problem.

My broader interest lies in understanding how language encodes movement, orientation and spatial knowledge.

thesis

⎈

Cognitive mechanisms of spatial conceptualization in ancient Greek periploi

BA thesis · Institute for Oriental and Classical Studies, HSE University · 2026

The thesis analyses three Greek periploi spanning almost nine centuries — Pseudo-Skylax, Arrian and Marcian of Heraclea — in order to reconstruct the cognitive principles underlying their descriptions of space.
